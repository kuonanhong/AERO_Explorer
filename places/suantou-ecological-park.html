土地的深呼吸：從廢耕地到紫色水境

The Land Breathes Again: From Fallow Cane Field to Purple Wetland

一塊台糖廢耕蔗田，如何變成淨水的池與夏天的千屈菜
How a fallow sugar-cane field became water-cleaning ponds ringed with summer loosestrife

旁白全文 Full narration

嘉義縣六腳鄉工廠村，朴子溪的堤防外側。介壽橋下這塊地，叫蒜頭生態公園。這裡原本是台糖的甘蔗田，後來廢耕。朴子溪分段整治時，它被劃進整治範圍。嘉義縣環保局投入約1800萬元，。在廢耕地上打造景觀生態池。池裡種的是水生植物。它們吸收水中的有機物和氨氮，水因此變乾淨。池岸那一圈紫色，是千屈菜。2020年，六腳鄉公所把它種在生態池邊。

千屈菜是多年生草本，株高40到120公分。它喜歡潮濕，原本就長在沼澤和水邊。資料記載，它原產於歐亞大陸的暖溫帶，。在臺灣也有人叫它千禧花。遠看像薰衣草，但它不是，也不是鼠尾草。花期很長，從6月開到10月。升高之後看得出來，公園的腹地不大，。兩座生態池也都不大。越過堤防，另一側就是朴子溪。堤頂有涼亭，居民早晚在這裡散步。

環境部在朴子溪設有水質測點，。介壽橋就是其中一處。溪水由東向西，從公園北側流過。河道和公園之間，隔著一道堤防。朴子溪發源於阿里山山脈，源頭海拔約1400公尺。全長將近76公里，向西流過嘉義。往下游，溪水經過朴子，。最後在東石鄉流入臺灣海峽。回到池邊。2010年曾有訪客記下：。生態池一帶雜草叢生。

十年後，池邊換成一整片紫色。往南望去，後方就是故宮南院的園區。公園裡沒有大樹遮蔭，。夏天的陽光直接落在水面和花穗上。從正上方看，紫紅色的花串圍著水面，。池水映出天空的顏色。一塊廢耕的蔗田，先成為淨水的池子，。又成為一片水邊的花地。人停止耕作之後，水和植物接手了這塊地。這是朴子溪畔的蒜頭生態公園。

Gongchang Village, Liujiao, Chiayi County, just outside the Puzi River levee. This plot beneath Jieshou Bridge is Suantou Ecological Park. It was once a Taiwan Sugar cane field that fell out of cultivation. It was then drawn into the section-by-section clean-up of the Puzi River. The county environmental bureau invested about NT$18 million to build landscape ecological ponds on the fallow land. The ponds are planted with aquatic plants. They take up organic matter and ammonia nitrogen, which cleans the water. The purple band around the shore is purple loosestrife. Liujiao Township Office planted it by the ponds in 2020.

Loosestrife is a perennial herb, 40 to 120 centimetres tall. It likes wet ground and grows naturally in marshes and along water. Sources describe it as native to warm-temperate Europe and Asia; in Taiwan it is also called the millennium flower. From a distance it resembles lavender, but it is neither lavender nor sage. It flowers for a long season, from June to October. From higher up, the park turns out to be a small site, and its two ecological ponds are small as well. Across the levee lies the Puzi River. A pavilion stands on the levee top, where residents walk morning and evening.

The Ministry of Environment keeps water-quality monitoring points on the Puzi River, and Jieshou Bridge is one of them. The river flows from east to west along the north side of the park. A levee separates the channel from the ponds. The Puzi River rises in the Alishan Range at about 1,400 metres. It runs nearly 76 kilometres westward across Chiayi. Downstream, the river passes Puzi and finally enters the Taiwan Strait in Dongshi Township. Back at the ponds. A visitor in 2010 noted that the ecological pond area was overgrown with weeds.

Ten years later, the pond edge had turned a solid band of purple. Looking south, the grounds of the Southern Branch museum lie just beyond. The park has no large trees for shade; summer sunlight falls straight onto the water and the flower spikes. From directly above, strings of purple-red flowers ring the water, and the pond reflects the colour of the sky. A fallow cane field first became ponds that clean water, and then a waterside flower ground. After farming stopped, water and plants took over this ground. This is Suantou Ecological Park, beside the Puzi River.

時光的軌道：五分車與紫海的平行時空

Tracks of Time: The Sugar Train and the Purple Sea, Side by Side

同一片蒜頭糖廠蔗田上，小火車與千屈菜各自走出的兩條時間線
Two timelines on the same Suantou cane fields: a narrow-gauge train and a pond of loosestrife

旁白全文 Full narration

蒜頭糖廠，位在朴子溪邊的六腳鄉工廠村。幾百公尺外，是夏天開滿紫花的生態公園。1906年，日本的明治製糖株式會社在這裡設廠。當時，它名列臺灣三大糖廠之一。廠區像一座自給自足的小鎮，。有附設小學、理髮店、打鐵店和鞋店。介壽堂建於1957年，是廠內的集會堂，。也曾經用來放映電影。廠區裡還有一座媽祖廟，。地方上稱祂「會社媽」或「糖廠媽」。

日本時代蓋的木造車站，已經整修並重新上漆。遊客在糖廠裡搭上五分車。糖業鐵道的軌距是762公釐，。約為標準軌的一半，這是「五分車」名稱的一種說法。全盛時期，全臺糖廠鐵道總長約3000公里，。比臺鐵的軌道還長。2001年9月，納莉颱風重創廠房和機具，。製糖從此停止。廠區後來轉型為蔗埕文化園區，。留下來的五分車改成載客。

2022年10月8日，。五分車的路線延伸到高鐵嘉義站。新鋪的軌道約1.4公里，全線3.4公里。這是全臺第一條與高鐵接駁的糖鐵路線。五分車時速只有十多公里，。在這裡和臺灣最快的列車並排。2023年10月7日，通往故宮南院的路線通車，。列車取名「尋蜜號」。故宮南院在2015年底開館，。基地過去是蒜頭糖廠的甘蔗田。

2023年12月，朴子配天宮媽祖遶境，。從故宮南院搭五分車到蒜頭糖廠。往北幾百公尺，另一塊台糖廢耕的蔗田，。成了蒜頭生態公園。池邊的紫色是千屈菜，夏天到秋天開花。蔗田退場之後，這裡多了一片紫色花海。旁邊的六家佃長壽橋2008年落成。這一帶，曾是台糖運蔗鐵道過溪的地方。2025年7月，丹娜絲颱風損壞橋上護欄，。橋因此封閉整修。

同一片蔗田上，軌道留下來載人，。田地讓給了水、花和博物館。

Suantou Sugar Factory stands in Gongchang Village, Liujiao, beside the Puzi River. A few hundred metres away lies an ecological park that turns purple in summer. In 1906 Japan's Meiji Sugar Company set up its factory here. It ranked among the three largest sugar factories in Taiwan at the time. The factory grounds worked like a self-contained town, with its own primary school, barber, blacksmith and shoe shop. Jieshou Hall, built in 1957, was the factory's assembly hall and also served for film screenings. There is also a Mazu temple inside the factory grounds; locals call the deity the Company Mazu or the Sugar Factory Mazu.

The Japanese-era wooden station building has been restored and repainted. Visitors board the narrow-gauge sugar train inside the factory. Sugar railways use a 762-millimetre gauge, about half of standard gauge, which is one explanation of the name "half-gauge train". At their peak, Taiwan's sugar railways totalled about 3,000 kilometres, more track than the national railway had. In September 2001 Typhoon Nari badly damaged the buildings and machinery, and sugar production stopped for good. The site was later turned into Zhecheng Cultural Park, and the surviving narrow-gauge trains began carrying passengers.

On 8 October 2022, the sugar train's line was extended to Chiayi High Speed Rail Station. About 1.4 kilometres of new track made a 3.4-kilometre line. It was the first sugar railway in Taiwan to connect with the high-speed rail. The sugar train runs at little more than 10 km/h, and here it stands beside the fastest trains in Taiwan. On 7 October 2023 the line to the Southern Branch museum opened, and the train was named Xunmi, the "honey seeker". The Southern Branch of the National Palace Museum opened at the end of 2015 on former cane fields of the Suantou Sugar Factory.

In December 2023 the touring Mazu of Puzi Peitian Temple rode the sugar train from the museum to the factory. A few hundred metres north, another fallow Taiwan Sugar cane field became Suantou Ecological Park. The purple by the ponds is loosestrife, in flower from summer into autumn. Where cane once grew, there is now a sea of purple. The nearby Liujiadian Changshou Bridge was completed in 2008. Taiwan Sugar's cane railway once crossed the river around here. In July 2025 Typhoon Danas damaged the bridge's railings, and it was closed for repairs.

On the same cane fields, the tracks stayed to carry people, while the land was given over to water, flowers and a museum.

從正上方往下看，嘉義六腳的蒜頭生態公園。這一段只看形狀、線條、顏色和倒影。拉高之後，池子的輪廓完整了。其中一座被稱作半月池，岸線是一道弧。千屈菜沿著池岸種成一圈，。紫色的花帶和水裡的倒影上下相對。沒有風的時候，水面像一面鏡子。天氣晴朗的日子，倒影最清楚。近看，花是一串一串的紫紅色長花穗，。和底下的綠葉分成上下兩層顏色。

堤頂的涼亭立在花海後方，。替橫向的畫面加上一個垂直的點。堤防是一條長線，。一邊是池塘和花，一邊是朴子溪的河道。介壽橋橫過溪面，是另一條直線。橋的另一頭，兩側的阿勃勒開花時一片金黃。紫、金、綠，加上水面映出的天藍，。幾種顏色在幾百公尺內排開。上游不遠處，六家佃長壽橋跨過朴子溪。兩座紅色高塔之間，拉著鋼纜。

這是一座自行車吊橋，全長390公尺。橋面是直線，纜索是弧線。從上方看，橋像一把尺，。橫放在兩岸之間。兩岸是雲嘉平原的田地，。一格一格，顏色深淺不同。朴子溪由東向西，。像一條帶子穿過田地之間。往南不遠，線條換了一種寫法。故宮南院的建築，取自水墨畫的三種筆法。

濃墨、飛白、渲染，。化成彼此交錯的流線量體，由姚仁喜設計。園區中央的人工湖分成兩部分，。南側是至善湖，北側是至德湖。跨湖的至美橋長142公尺，是單跨鋼拱橋，。由25道弧形拱肋組成。東邊的蒜頭糖廠裡，。五分車的窄軌是這片平原上最細的線。東南邊，高鐵的高架橋是最長的一條直線，。嘉義站就在這條線上。

弧形的池、筆直的堤、交錯的橋與建築，。都落在朴子溪邊這一小塊平原上。

Looking straight down on Suantou Ecological Park in Liujiao, Chiayi. This flight is only about shapes, lines, colours and reflections. Higher up, the outline of the ponds comes together. One is known as the Half-Moon Pond; its shore is a single arc. Purple loosestrife is planted in a ring along the shore, so the band of flowers faces its own reflection in the water. When the wind drops, the water becomes a mirror. On fine days the reflections are at their clearest. Up close, the flowers are long purple-red spikes, forming a layer of colour above the green leaves below.

The pavilion on the levee rises behind the flowers, adding one vertical accent to a horizontal picture. The levee is one long line: ponds and flowers on one side, the Puzi River channel on the other. Jieshou Bridge crosses the river as another straight line. At its far end, golden shower trees on both sides turn yellow in bloom. Purple, gold and green, plus the sky-blue held by the water: a handful of colours laid out within a few hundred metres. A short way upstream, Liujiadian Changshou Bridge spans the Puzi River. Steel cables hang between its two tall red towers.

It is a suspension bridge for bicycles, 390 metres long. The deck is a straight line; the cables are curves. From above, the bridge looks like a ruler laid across from one bank to the other. On both banks lie the fields of the Yunlin-Chiayi plain, plot after plot in differing shades. The Puzi River runs from east to west, like a ribbon threading between the fields. A short way south, the lines are written differently. The Southern Branch museum draws on three brush techniques of ink painting.

Thick ink, flying white and wash became interlocking streamlined volumes, designed by Kris Yao. The artificial lake at the centre of the grounds has two parts: Zhishan Lake to the south and Zhide Lake to the north. Zhimei Bridge crosses the lake in a single 142-metre steel arch span, made up of 25 curved ribs. In the Suantou Sugar Factory to the east, the narrow-gauge rails are the thinnest lines on this plain. To the south-east, the high-speed rail viaduct is the longest straight line of all, and Chiayi Station sits on it.

The arc of a pond, a straight levee, bridges and buildings that cross and weave: all of it sits on one small piece of plain beside the Puzi River.

The Frog Who Looked for Light: An Eco-Diary of a Summer Night and Dawn

我是一隻只住在故事裡的小青蛙，這是我的日記。傍晚，蒜頭生態公園的池邊開始變涼。公園裡沒有大樹，白天很曬。寫遊記的人建議，傍晚五六點再來。堤防上有涼亭。附近的居民，早晚都沿著堤頂散步。池邊的紫色花叫千屈菜。夕陽把花穗和水面染成同一種顏色。故事裡，我的家有兩座池子，。一座叫生態池，一座叫景觀池。

太陽往朴子溪下游的方向落下去。公園全天開放，夜裡沒有關門的時刻。天黑以後，我往有光的地方看。六家佃長壽橋設有夜間照明，橫在溪面上。旅遊報導說，橋燈從傍晚六點半亮到清晨五點。不過2025年颱風後橋曾封閉整修，燈未必每晚都亮。東邊的糖廠早就不製糖了，。載客的五分車，要等白天才出發。往南看，故宮南院就在幾百公尺外。夜裡，我只認得出它的輪廓。

月光下，池水比岸邊亮。紫色看不見了，花只剩下深色的影子。到了夜裡，水生植物不行光合作用，。只是安靜地呼吸。真正的無人機，日落後到日出前不能飛，。除非事先申請核准；這一段只存在模擬器裡。天從糖廠那一邊亮起來，。先是灰藍，再是橘色。第一道光貼著水面過來，。千屈菜的顏色一點一點回來。

這種花從夏天一直開到秋天，。所以每個清晨，我都看到同一片紫色。堤頂上，早起散步的人來了。介壽橋那頭，新的一天開始。天亮以後，植物又開始行光合作用。這座池子，是為了淨化朴子溪的水而設的。升高一點，看得到朴子溪、糖廠和故宮南院。我的池子，只是其中很小的一塊。日記裡的青蛙是虛構的。池邊真正住著哪些動物，要實地調查才知道。

一個夏夜過去，光從橋燈、月亮換成太陽。蒜頭生態公園的清晨，日記寫到這裡。

I am a small frog who lives only in this story, and this is my diary. Evening: the pond edge at Suantou Ecological Park begins to cool. The park has no big trees, so the days are hot. Travel writers suggest coming at five or six in the evening. There is a pavilion up on the levee. People who live nearby walk along the levee top morning and evening. The purple flowers by the pond are called loosestrife. The setting sun tints the flower spikes and the water the same colour. In the story, my home has two ponds: one called the ecological pond, the other the landscape pond.

The sun goes down toward the lower reaches of the Puzi River. The park is open around the clock; there is no closing time at night. After dark I look toward wherever there is light. Liujiadian Changshou Bridge, fitted with night lighting, lies across the river. A travel report says the bridge lights run from 6:30 in the evening until 5 in the morning. But the bridge was closed for repairs after a 2025 typhoon, so they may not shine every night. The sugar factory to the east stopped making sugar long ago; its passenger trains do not set out until daytime. To the south, the Southern Branch museum is only a few hundred metres away. At night I can make out nothing but its outline.

Under the moon, the pond is brighter than its banks. The purple is gone; the flowers are only dark shapes. At night the aquatic plants stop photosynthesising and simply breathe, quietly. A real drone may not fly between sunset and sunrise without prior approval; this stretch exists only in the simulator. The sky brightens from the direction of the sugar factory, grey-blue first, then orange. The first light comes in low across the water, and the colour of the loosestrife returns little by little.

These flowers keep blooming from summer into autumn, so every dawn I find the same field of purple. Up on the levee, the early walkers have arrived. Over by Jieshou Bridge, a new day begins. With daylight, the plants begin to photosynthesise again. This pond was built to help clean the water of the Puzi River. A little higher, the Puzi River, the sugar factory and the museum come into view. My pond is only a very small piece of it all. The frog in this diary is made up. Which animals really live by the pond can only be learned from a field survey.

A summer night has passed; the light has moved from bridge lamps and moon to the sun. Morning at Suantou Ecological Park: the diary ends here.
