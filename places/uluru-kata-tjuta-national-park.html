歷史 History

歸還的土地

Land Handed Back

1873年的命名、1985年的歸還，到2019年停止攀登
A name in 1873, the handback of 1985, and the end of climbing in 2019

旁白全文 Full narration

數萬年來，阿南古人生活在這片土地上。烏魯魯與卡塔丘塔，是他們的家園與聖地。1872年，探險家歐內斯特·賈爾斯看到卡塔丘塔。他應植物學家米勒的請求，以符騰堡王后奧爾加命名為奧爾加山。1873年7月19日，測量員威廉·高斯看見烏魯魯。他以南澳首席秘書亨利·艾爾斯之名，稱它為艾爾斯岩。從此，這塊巨岩有了兩個名字。一個來自阿南古人，一個來自殖民者。阿南古人和土地委員會長年爭取土地權。1983年上任的霍克政府，終於宣布改變政策。

1985年10月26日，總督史蒂芬在岩石下交出公園地契。兩千多人見證了這一刻。阿南古人隨即把公園租回給國家，租期99年。公園由阿南古人佔多數的委員會共同管理。1987年，公園因自然價值列入世界遺產。1994年，又以文化景觀的身分再次登錄。它是世界上第二座以文化景觀列入世界遺產的國家公園。登錄的理由，是人與土地之間的關係。1993年起，官方採用雙重名稱。2002年，順序改成「烏魯魯／艾爾斯岩」。

2017年11月1日，公園委員會決定禁止攀登。2019年10月26日起，正式停止攀登烏魯魯。理由包括岩石對阿南古人的精神意義，。攀登路線穿過一條神聖的路徑，以及遊客的安全。從被命名、被歸還，到不再被攀登，。這塊土地的歷史，回到了它原本的主人手中。

For tens of thousands of years the Aṉangu have lived on this land. Uluṟu and Kata Tjuṯa are their home and their sacred places. In 1872 the explorer Ernest Giles sighted Kata Tjuṯa. At botanist von Mueller's request he named it Mount Olga, after Queen Olga of Württemberg. On 19 July 1873 the surveyor William Gosse sighted Uluṟu and named it Ayers Rock, after South Australia's Chief Secretary, Sir Henry Ayers. From then on the rock had two names, one from the Aṉangu and one from the colonists. The Aṉangu and the land councils campaigned for years for their land. The Hawke government, elected in 1983, finally announced a change.

On 26 October 1985 Governor-General Sir Ninian Stephen handed over the title deeds at the foot of the rock, before more than 2,000 people. The Aṉangu then leased the park back to the nation for 99 years, to be jointly managed by a board with an Aṉangu majority. In 1987 the park joined the World Heritage List for its natural values, and in 1994 it was listed again as a cultural landscape. It was the second national park in the world to be listed as a cultural landscape, recognising the bond between people and land. A dual-naming policy began in 1993, and in 2002 the order became Uluṟu / Ayers Rock.

On 1 November 2017 the park board voted to ban climbing, and from 26 October 2019 the climb was closed. Reasons included the rock's spiritual importance to the Aṉangu, a climbing route crossing a sacred track, and visitor safety. From naming to handback to the end of the climb, the story of this land has returned to its first custodians.

人文 Culture

楚庫帕：土地的律法

Tjukurpa: the Law of the Land

阿南古人的知識、岩畫與水潭，以及給旅人的請求
Aṉangu knowledge, rock art and waterholes, and a request to visitors

對阿南古人來說，烏魯魯不只是一塊岩石。它承載著「楚庫帕」，一套連結人與土地的信仰與律法。楚庫帕說明了世界如何形成，也規範人該如何生活。公園的管理，以楚庫帕為核心。岩石南側的穆蒂圖魯水潭，是沙漠中珍貴的水源。岩石周圍有泉水、水潭、岩洞與古老的岩畫。岩畫與岩刻，記錄著代代相傳的故事。這些故事，透過歌、舞與畫流傳下來。文化中心由阿南古人參與規劃，介紹他們的文化。公園的解說，以阿南古人的知識為主。

岩石底部有些地方是神聖的場所。阿南古人請遊客不要拍攝這些地點，現場會有標示。黃昏時，遊客聚在觀景區，看岩石轉紅。紅色的岩石，是澳洲最熟悉的風景之一。2000年時，每年的遊客已超過四十萬人。大多數人住在公園外的尤拉拉度假村。尤拉拉位在公園北方。從這裡，可以同時望見烏魯魯和卡塔丘塔。卡塔丘塔的名字，意思是「許多頭」。這裡也是阿南古人的聖地。

風之谷步道，讓遊客走進圓頂之間。炎熱的日子，部分步道會封閉。攀登停止後，遊客改為繞著岩石底部步行。近距離看見水潭與岩洞，也聽見阿南古人的故事。尊重這片土地的方式，就是聽它的主人怎麼說。在烏魯魯，旅人也成了楚庫帕故事的一部分。

To the Aṉangu, Uluṟu is not just a rock. It holds Tjukurpa, a religious philosophy linking people to their land. Tjukurpa explains how the world came to be and how people should live. It is at the centre of how the park is managed. Mutitjulu Waterhole on the south side is a precious source of water. Around the rock are springs, waterholes, caves and ancient paintings. Paintings and carvings record stories passed down the generations, carried on in song, dance and art. The Cultural Centre, shaped with the Aṉangu, introduces their culture. Their knowledge is the main voice in explaining the park.

Some places at the base of the rock are sacred. The Aṉangu ask visitors not to photograph them; signs mark where. At dusk visitors gather at the viewing area to watch the rock turn red, one of Australia's most familiar sights. By 2000 more than 400,000 people were visiting each year, most of them staying at Yulara outside the park. Yulara lies north of the park. From here both Uluṟu and Kata Tjuṯa can be seen. Kata Tjuṯa means "many heads". It too is a sacred place for the Aṉangu.

The Valley of the Winds walk leads in among the domes; parts of it close on very hot days. Since the climb closed, visitors walk around the base instead, seeing the waterholes and caves up close and hearing Aṉangu stories. To respect this land is to listen to its custodians. At Uluṟu, visitors become part of the story of Tjukurpa.

自然 Nature

紅色中心的生命

Life in the Red Centre

雨水從巨岩流下、沙原與沙丘，以及沙漠動植物
Rain running off the rock, sand plains and dunes, and desert life

烏魯魯一帶是乾燥的沙漠，雨水稀少。紅色的沙原上，長著耐旱的草與灌木。下雨時，雨水沿著岩面迅速流下。巨岩把雨水集中到底部的水潭。大雨時，岩面上會出現一道道短暫的瀑布。雨停之後，瀑布很快就消失。穆蒂圖魯水潭，在乾季也常常有水。水潭周圍，聚集著鳥類與其他動物。岩石底部的陰影與水分，讓植物長得比平原茂盛。巨岩，在沙漠中創造了一個小小的綠洲。

沙原上長著一叢叢的鬣刺草。它尖銳的葉子，能減少水分散失。沙丘之間，長著高大的沙漠橡木。它們的根，能深入地下尋找水。卡塔丘塔的谷地比較陰涼。雨後，谷裡的溪流和水潭會短暫出現。圓頂之間的谷地，是動物躲避酷熱的地方。許多動物在清晨和黃昏才出來活動。沙漠中住著許多爬蟲類，例如身上長滿尖刺的棘蜥。牠們適應了白天炎熱、夜晚寒冷的環境。

澳洲野犬、袋鼠與各種鳥類，也在沙原上活動。牠們多半在比較涼爽的時段覓食。阿南古人用火來照顧土地。小規模的燒除，能讓植物更新，也減少大火的風險。公園的管理，結合了傳統知識與現代科學。這片看似空曠的沙漠，其實充滿生命。遠離城市的沙漠，夜空幾乎沒有光害。天黑之後，銀河清楚地橫過天空。雨水從巨岩流下，養活了水潭邊的生命；。在紅色的中心，每一滴水都很珍貴。

The country around Uluṟu is dry desert with little rain. Hardy grasses and shrubs grow on the red sand plain. When it rains, water pours quickly down the rock face. The rock gathers it into the waterholes at its foot. In heavy rain, short-lived waterfalls stream down the rock. Soon after the rain stops, they are gone. Mutitjulu Waterhole often holds water even in the dry season, drawing birds and other animals around it. Shade and moisture at the base let plants grow thicker than on the plain. The rock makes a small oasis in the desert.

Clumps of spinifex grass dot the sand plain, their spiky leaves cutting water loss. Tall desert oaks grow between the dunes, their roots reaching deep for water. Kata Tjuṯa's valleys are cooler and more sheltered. After rain, creeks and pools appear there for a while. The valleys between the domes are refuges from the heat. Many animals come out only at dawn and dusk. Many reptiles live in this desert, such as the spiny thorny devil, adapted to scorching days and cold nights.

Dingoes, kangaroos and many birds also range across the plain, mostly feeding in the cooler hours. The Aṉangu use fire to care for the country. Small burns renew the plants and lower the risk of big fires. Park management combines traditional knowledge with modern science. This seemingly empty desert is full of life. Far from any city, the desert night is almost free of light pollution. After dark the Milky Way arches clearly overhead. Rain running off the rock feeds the life around its waterholes; in the Red Centre, every drop counts.

地理 Geography

沙漠中的兩座巨岩

Two Great Rocks in the Desert

五億多年前的沉積、翻轉的岩層，與36座圓頂
Sediments over 500 million years old, upended layers, and 36 domes

旁白全文 Full narration

澳洲中部的沙漠裡，突起一塊巨岩：烏魯魯。它比周圍平原高出348公尺，海拔863公尺。它的周長約9.4公里。位置在愛麗斯泉西南方約335公里。烏魯魯主要由長石砂岩構成。這種砂岩含有大量長石，形成於約五億五千萬到五億三千萬年前。岩層原本是水平沉積的。後來地殼運動，把它們翻轉到幾乎垂直。仔細看岩壁上一條條平行的紋路，。那就是被翻轉過來的岩層。

我們看到的，只是岩體的一小部分。大部分岩層，延伸到平原底下。岩石裡的氧化鐵，讓它呈現紅色。清晨與黃昏，岩石的顏色會跟著陽光改變。往西25公里，是另一群巨岩：卡塔丘塔。中間是一片平坦的沙原。卡塔丘塔由36座圓頂組成，分布在約21.7平方公里內。它的岩石是礫岩：卵石與巨石被砂岩膠結在一起。最高的奧爾加山，比平原高出546公尺。它比烏魯魯還高198公尺。

圓頂之間，是深深的谷地。風之谷步道，就從圓頂之間穿過。卡塔丘塔的岩層只微微傾斜，。和幾乎垂直的烏魯魯很不一樣。從正上方看，36座圓頂像一群擠在一起的頭。卡塔丘塔的意思，正是「許多頭」。兩群巨岩周圍，是幾乎平坦的沙原。較軟的岩石早已被侵蝕，只剩下最堅硬的部分。五億年的沉積、翻轉、抬升與侵蝕，。留下了沙漠中這兩座巨岩。

In the desert of central Australia rises a great rock: Uluṟu. It stands 348 metres above the plain, 863 metres above sea level. It is about 9.4 kilometres around, some 335 kilometres south-west of Alice Springs. Uluṟu is mostly arkose, a sandstone rich in feldspar, laid down some 550 to 530 million years ago. The layers were first laid down flat. Later movements of the crust tipped them almost on end. Look closely at the parallel ribs on the rock face: they are those upended layers.

What we see is only a small part of the formation. Most of it continues beneath the plain. Iron oxide gives the rock its red colour. At dawn and dusk its hue changes with the light. Twenty-five kilometres to the west lies another group of rocks: Kata Tjuṯa. Between them stretches a flat sand plain. Kata Tjuṯa is 36 domes spread over about 21.7 square kilometres. Its rock is conglomerate: cobbles and boulders cemented by sandstone. The highest dome, Mount Olga, rises 546 metres above the plain, 198 metres higher than Uluṟu.

Deep valleys run between the domes. The Valley of the Winds walk threads its way through them. Kata Tjuṯa's layers are only gently tilted, quite unlike the near-vertical beds of Uluṟu. From straight above, the 36 domes look like heads crowded together. Kata Tjuṯa means "many heads". Around both groups the sand plain is almost flat. Softer rock wore away long ago, leaving only the hardest parts. Five hundred million years of deposition, folding, uplift and erosion have left these two great rocks in the desert.

實際飛行前 Before a real flight

烏魯魯－卡塔丘塔國家公園是「無人機禁飛公園」：操作無人機須事先取得公園許可，且只有在特殊情況、並完成中央土地委員會的諮詢後才可能核准；為商業或公開目的拍攝烏魯魯或卡塔丘塔也須許可。實際飛行前請先確認澳洲民航安全局與公園的規定。

Uluṟu-Kata Tjuṯa is a no-drone park: operating a drone needs a park permit, approved only in exceptional circumstances after consultation with the Central Land Council; depicting Uluṟu or Kata Tjuṯa for commercial or public purposes also needs a permit.
